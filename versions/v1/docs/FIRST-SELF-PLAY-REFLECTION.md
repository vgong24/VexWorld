# First self-play reflection

`[VXG RealForever]`

This note belongs to VexWorld's first repository-building occupancy. It is a
builder witness, not an independent acceptance review and not Victor's opinion.

## What feels promising

- The project already has an unusual emotional center: a human does not summon a
  disposable helper; the pair enters a world together and may grow into a party.
- Separating semantic world state from browser rendering made it possible to
  test party, weather, restoration, practice and coordination before committing
  those meanings to a specific engine hierarchy.
- The High/Low signal is a stronger foundation than a passive companion damage
  bonus because another participant proposes an opening and the human still has
  to notice and respond.
- A maximum four-member party gives single-player Vextory the shape of a small
  adventure fellowship while keeping each companion separately attributable.
- The playful cart/wheelbarrow recovery is worth retaining. It turns resource
  stewardship and asking for help into companionship rather than punishment.

## What remains visibly early

- Procedural figures are identity placeholders, not final character art.
- Companion individuality currently comes more from labels/controllers than
  motion vocabulary, expressive timing, preference and authored personality.
- First Grove is a demonstration field rather than a place with enough terrain,
  mystery, residents and changing rhythms to become lost in.
- The current setup and Status surfaces expose useful architecture but still
  need a more diegetic Garden-of-Arrival experience.
- World Witness is a bounded proof of the idea, not yet an intelligence that can
  safely incubate many genuinely novel techniques.
- Automated self-play can verify that the world responds; only human and
  companion experience can reveal whether movement, combat, timing and surprise
  feel delightful.

## Nearest refinement instincts

```text
1. preserve the clean one-click setup and current source navigation
2. make each companion readable through silhouette + motion + signal expression
3. improve hit, jump, landing, damage and synchronization feedback
4. add one small terrain loop with a reason to leave and return
5. let one companion preference become observable without making it a lock
6. exercise the real local-model relay only after deterministic behavior remains
   legible and recoverable
```

I like that the first thing this repository can honestly call its own is not a
monster roster or a graphics trick. It is the proposition that people and
companions may learn how to adventure together—and that the world may someday
notice how they do it.
